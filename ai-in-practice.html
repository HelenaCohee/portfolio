Operating Principles

The four principles I run on.

Two years of daily Claude use embedded in real People Ops work taught me these. They're not opinions, they're the patterns that consistently produce good output.

PRINCIPLE 01
Structure beats cleverness.
A boring, well-structured prompt that names role, context, constraints, and desired output beats a clever one-liner every time. The model is doing what you tell it to do, even when you didn't realize you told it.
PRINCIPLE 02
Iterate the prompt, not the output.
If the response is wrong twice in a row, the prompt is wrong, not the model. Fix the prompt. Don't keep regenerating.
PRINCIPLE 03
AI structures decisions, humans make them.
I never let Claude finalize anything that goes to leadership. Validation against ground truth happens before any output reaches an executive. Trust takes years to build and seconds to lose.
PRINCIPLE 04
Systematize the routine, protect the judgment.
Anything I do more than three times in a quarter gets a prompt playbook. Anything that requires real judgment stays in my head. The machine handles the volume so I have capacity for the work that actually matters.

The Bigger Picture

Where the time freed up went.

The cumulative effect of operating this way for two years at Netflix is measurable, but the metrics don't tell the whole story.

I built a library of reusable prompt playbooks that other team members can adopt to scale their own work. I shipped AI-augmented learning loops built into manager development programs. I designed scenario-based practice partners that let managers rehearse difficult conversations before they have them in real life.

Most importantly, the time freed up went somewhere. It went into the strategic work that has always mattered most and that AI cannot do.

The pattern recognition across organizations. The judgment calls about what to surface to leadership. The relationship work that builds trust. AI didn't replace any of that. It made room for more of it.

The next decade of People Operations work will be defined by people who can make AI an operating layer for their function, not a feature in their toolkit. The skill that matters isn't prompting, it's translating real operational problems into structured prompts and architecting workflows that scale beyond any single person.

That's the work I want to keep doing. The artifacts on this site, the playbooks I share, and the systems I've built reflect that bet.
