AI in Practice

How I operate with AI, not just how I use it
Most People Operations and Talent Management teams treat AI as a productivity gadget. I treat it as an operating layer. The difference shows up in the work.

My Operating Stack

I run a multi-tool AI workflow because each tool has a distinct role. The right tool for the right job is its own kind of fluency.

Claude
Primary tool. Daily use for synthesis, executive-level communications, problem structuring, and rapid prototyping. Where my reusable prompt playbooks live.
ChatGPT
Cross-model comparison. When stakes are high, I run the same prompt across models and triangulate. Different models have different blind spots.
Manus
Coding and document template generation. Used to scaffold the technical infrastructure I've shipped, including production code and document systems.
Magnus AI
Meeting transcription and synthesis. Captures verbatim, generates structured summaries, surfaces action items I'd otherwise have to extract manually.

The Four Principles I Run On

Structure beats cleverness.

A boring, well-structured prompt that names role, context, constraints, and desired output beats a clever one-liner every time. The model is doing what you tell it to do, even when you didn't realize you told it.

Iterate the prompt, not the output.

If the response is wrong twice in a row, the prompt is wrong, not the model. Fix the prompt. Don't keep regenerating.

AI structures decisions, humans make them.

I never let Claude finalize anything that goes to leadership. Validation against ground truth happens before any output reaches an executive. Trust takes years to build and seconds to lose.

Systematize the routine, protect the judgment.

Anything I do more than three times in a quarter gets a prompt playbook. Anything that requires real judgment stays in my head. The machine handles the volume so I have capacity for the work that actually matters.

What This Has Made Possible

The cumulative effect of operating this way for two years at Netflix:

30% reduction in manual operational effort across enablement workflows. 60 hours of quarterly reporting eliminated through dashboard automation paired with Claude synthesis. A library of reusable prompt playbooks that other team members can adopt to scale their own work. AI-augmented learning loops built into manager development programs achieving 89% activation, 4.6/5 satisfaction, and measurable behavior change at 90 days post-program.

Most importantly, the time freed up went somewhere. It went into the strategic work that has always mattered most and that AI cannot do. The pattern recognition across organizations. The judgment calls about what to surface to leadership. The relationship work that builds trust. AI didn't replace any of that. It made room for more of it.

The Larger Bet

The next decade of People Operations work will be defined by people who can make AI an operating layer for their function, not a feature in their toolkit. The skill that matters isn't prompting, it's translating real operational problems into structured prompts and architecting workflows that scale beyond any single person.

That's the work I want to keep doing. The artifacts on this site, the playbooks I share, and the systems I've built reflect that bet.
